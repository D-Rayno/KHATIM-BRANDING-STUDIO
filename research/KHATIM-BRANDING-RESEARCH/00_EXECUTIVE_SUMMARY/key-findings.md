# Key Findings

## Finding 01 — The category is shifting from signing to trust
**Fact:** EU eIDAS 2 now covers a broad set of trust services and wallet-related interfaces. **Interpretation:** a signing brand designed today should not be visually or verbally locked to a signature field.

## Finding 02 — “Trust” is table stakes, not differentiation
Competitors repeatedly claim security, compliance, speed, ease and legal validity. These are purchase prerequisites. Khatim needs a sharper idea.

## Finding 03 — Khatim’s strongest semantic asset may be “completion”
The root ختم connects sealing/imprinting with completion/end. **Hypothesis:** Khatim can credibly own the moment an uncertain digital action becomes final, evidenced and accepted.

## Finding 04 — Cultural heritage can be strategic
Historical seals attached identity to documents and objects, preserving ownership/provenance. This is much more interesting than using Arabic ornaments or generic geometric patterns.

## Finding 05 — The name itself is currently the largest risk
There is a same-category Khatim product family from Codegic. This could affect trademark clearance, searchability, SEO, market confusion and future brand ownership.

## Finding 06 — Regional markets already understand “digital trust” as an infrastructure category
UAE, Saudi Arabia and Morocco show regulatory or commercial ecosystems around trust services, certificates, digital seals, PKI and legally recognized digital signatures.

## Finding 07 — The strongest whitespace is between legal/institutional trust and modern product experience
Legacy trust-service vendors often feel technical and infrastructure-heavy; e-signature platforms often feel workflow/product-led. Khatim can potentially sit between these poles: **institutional proof expressed through a modern, human digital experience.**
