## Phase 3 — Visual Identity Exploration: Symbol Family Study

> **STATUS: EXPLORATION ONLY.** Every family, score, and SVG in this document is a STUDY / PROTOTYPE / CONCEPT — not a logo candidate, not a final mark. Per `strategy/14-PHASE-3-GATE.md`, nothing here may be locked.

## 1. Method

1. **Semantic field mapping.** I exploded the strategic truth into its component meanings — witness, attestation, imprint, matrix, completion, permanence, proof, neutral third party, cryptographic relationship, state transition, evidence, authority — and treated each as a *generative axis*, not a styling brief.
2. **One mechanism per family.** A family is defined by its **semiotic mechanism** (how the mark produces meaning), not by its surface style. Two families may share geometry (e.g., octagons) but must differ in mechanism. This forces genuine diversity instead of cosmetic variation.
3. **Constraint filter.** Anything relying on shields, locks, keys, checkmarks, pen strokes, circuit boards, blue security gradients, literal wax seals, signet rings/jewelry, or decorative Arabic/Islamic ornament was rejected at sketch stage (see §3).
4. **Hierarchy test.** Each family was checked against the locked hierarchy: does it lead with Witness/Attestation (PRIMARY), can the 45° rhombic *nuqta* grid act as subterranean structure (SECONDARY), and does it stay out of the forensic-UI lane (PRODUCT BEHAVIOR)?
5. **Name-independence test.** Every family must function under a fallback name (e.g., "Nexus Trust") with zero loss of meaning.
6. **Evaluation.** Each family scored on the mandated 13 axes. Memorability scored 1–5 with justification.
7. **SVG studies.** The 6 strongest hypotheses were drawn as rough, clean, monochrome vector studies in `design/phase-3/svg/`.

## 2. The 16 Symbol Families

Format per family: **Concept** · **Mechanism** · **Geometry** · **Negative space** · **Construction** · **Memorability (1–5)** · **Scalability** · **16px** · **Monochrome** · **Cultural** · **International** · **Copyability** · **Risks**

### F-01 — The Pressed Nuqta (Deboss Field)
- **Concept:** A field of identical 45° rhombic units in which exactly one cell has been pressed into a solid, displaced state. The event — the attestation — is the single altered cell.
- **Mechanism:** *Displacement within a uniform field.* Meaning arises from the contrast between untouched matrix and the one permanent impression. This is the deboss made systematic: the grid is the substrate, the pressed cell is the public proof.
- **Geometry:** 45° rhombi (nuqta units) on a regular lattice; one filled rhombus, the rest hairline outlines.
- **Negative space:** The outline cells are mostly void; the pressed cell is the only solid — figure/ground inversion carries the whole idea.
- **Construction:** Built directly on the subterranean nuqta grid; the grid is simultaneously the proportion system and the mark itself.
- **Memorability: 5** — one-gesture idea, describable in five words ("the grid with one pressed dot"); survives retelling.
- **Scalability:** Excellent upward (grid can extend infinitely for environmental graphics); downward requires cell-count reduction.
- **16px:** Reduces to a 3×3 or 2×2 dot field with one solid dot — still legible, still on-concept.
- **Monochrome:** Native. The entire idea is solid-vs-outline; color adds nothing.
- **Cultural:** The nuqta is the classical Arabic calligraphic unit of measure — the heritage moat is embedded structurally and invisibly, exactly as T-04 demands. No ornament.
- **International:** Reads as pure system/geometry in the West; reads as quietly rooted in MENA. No orientalist surface.
- **Copyability:** LOW — cloning requires adopting the entire displacement logic, not just a shape. DocuSign could draw a dot grid, but not the *reason* one cell is pressed.
- **Risks:** (a) At very small sizes the outline cells may fill in visually; (b) could be misread as a "pixel/braille" tech mark if the rhombic angle is lost; (c) dot-grid adjacency to generic "digital" aesthetics must be managed through the 45° angle discipline.

### F-02 — Matrix & Impression (Dual-State Pair)
- **Concept:** Two identical octagonal forms in vertical relation: the upper solid (the die / private key), the lower a hollow outline (the deboss / public key). The mark is the *relationship*, not either object.
- **Mechanism:** *Positive/negative dual-state pairing.* The most literal viable translation of asymmetric cryptography into form — presence above, absence below, same geometry.
- **Geometry:** Faceted octagons (signet-bezel adjacency without jewelry), aligned on a vertical axis; optional hairline of contact between them.
- **Negative space:** The lower form is pure void bounded by stroke — the impression as emptiness with evidence of pressure.
- **Construction:** Octagon derived from compass construction on the nuqta grid; vertical spacing on grid units.
- **Memorability: 4** — "solid shape over empty shape" is describable and ownable, but two-part marks are inherently harder to recall than one-gesture marks.
- **Scalability:** Good; the pair compresses to a single solid-over-outline glyph at small sizes.
- **16px:** Survives as a filled dot over a ring — concept degrades gracefully but the octagonal faceting disappears.
- **Monochrome:** Native — the idea *is* fill vs. stroke.
- **Cultural:** Die/impression is the historical signet logic (T-04) rendered as pure physics, not artifact. Safe.
- **International:** Universal; the press/imprint relationship is understood across all markets (seals East and West).
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM — the *concept* is ownable, but solid-over-outline pairs are simple to approximate; defensibility rests on proportion discipline and the octagonal facet language.
- **Risks:** (a) Vertical pair can read as a "stamp about to stamp" — skeuomorph adjacency; (b) two-element marks complicate horizontal lockups; (c) risks being read as an exclamation mark at tiny sizes.

### F-03 — Tripartite Convergence
- **Concept:** Three strokes converge from three directions and, at the meeting point, form a small triangular void — the attested state that only exists because all three parties (A, B, Witness) arrived.
- **Mechanism:** *Convergence into a locked third form.* The triangle at the center is the triadic trust structure (T-02's Tripartite Axis) made geometric: the witness is what the convergence creates.
- **Geometry:** Three strokes at 120° or asymmetric angles; central equilateral triangle as negative or outlined space.
- **Negative space:** The central void is the payload — meaning lives where the strokes *don't* go.
- **Construction:** Radii from an equilateral triangle's vertices, extended outward on grid axes.
- **Memorability: 3** — elegant, but three-lines-meeting is a well-worn tech gesture (convergence, network, merger marks).
- **Scalability:** Excellent; pure stroke geometry.
- **16px:** Good — three strokes + center dot read clearly.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral; triadic structure resonates with shahadah (witness) logic without depicting it.
- **International:** Universal; convergence is globally legible — which is also its weakness.
- **Copyability:** HIGH — the easiest family on this list for a competitor to approximate. Only exceptional proportion/angle discipline would defend it.
- **Risks:** (a) Mercedes/peace-sign adjacency at 120° symmetry — asymmetric angles required; (b) generic "partnership" reading dilutes the *impartial arbiter* nuance; (c) crowding the most contested visual territory in tech.

### F-04 — The Keystone
- **Concept:** Two opposed arch segments held in permanent compression by a central trapezoidal keystone. Remove the witness and the structure fails.
- **Mechanism:** *Load-bearing third element.* Architectural argument for the impartial third party: the keystone does not decorate the arch, it *is* the arch's stability.
- **Geometry:** Trapezoid + two curved or faceted arch segments; bilateral symmetry.
- **Negative space:** The arch void beneath; the keystone as solid apex.
- **Construction:** Classical arch geometry regularized onto the rhombic grid (faceted, not curved, to avoid heritage-ornament adjacency).
- **Memorability: 3** — meaningful but "keystone" is a used metaphor (Pennsylvania, Keystone banks, architecture firms).
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Weak — arch segments collapse into blobs; keystone reads as a plain trapezoid.
- **Monochrome:** Good.
- **Cultural:** Roman/Western architectural coding; weaker MENA resonance but not hostile.
- **International:** Strong in US/EU institutional contexts; neutral elsewhere.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM-HIGH — keystones exist in financial/institutional branding already.
- **Risks:** (a) Reads "architecture/engineering firm" before "trust infrastructure"; (b) static, inert feeling conflicts with state-transition energy; (c) weak favicon.

### F-05 — The Void Core
- **Concept:** A single heavy, faceted mass whose entire meaning is the precise diamond-shaped absence cut from its center. The deboss abstracted to its essence: *the mark is what was displaced.*
- **Mechanism:** *Meaningful void.* Figure-ground reversal where the hole — not the mass — is the signifier. The negative impression as pure negative space.
- **Geometry:** Solid octagon (or square) perimeter; centered 45° rhombic void.
- **Negative space:** Total — the void is the logo's subject; the mass is merely its substrate.
- **Construction:** Outer contour and inner void both constructed on the nuqta grid; void sized as an exact grid unit.
- **Memorability: 5** — a block with a precise hole is maximally reducible and survives verbal description perfectly.
- **Scalability:** Superb — two nested polygons, nothing to lose.
- **16px:** Excellent — arguably the best favicon behavior of all 16 families: solid octagon, bright diamond hole.
- **Monochrome:** Native and perfect.
- **Cultural:** The rhombic void embeds the nuqta invisibly; mass-and-void echoes carved seal matrices without depicting one.
- **International:** Completely neutral; no cultural pre-loading in any market.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM — hole-in-solid exists in corporate marks (bank/industrial logos); defensibility depends on the specific octagon+rhombus proportion system and its tie to the grid story.
- **Risks:** (a) May read as abstract to the point of muteness — the witness narrative must be carried by system behavior (motion: the void "pressing in"); (b) "O"/zero adjacency; (c) requires disciplined proportions to avoid generic app-icon look.

### F-06 — The Threshold (State-Transition Line)
- **Concept:** A single horizontal line; a form crossing it changes state — outline above (asserted), solid below (attested). The line is the moment of witnessing.
- **Mechanism:** *Irreversible state transition across a boundary.* The witness act as a before/after register.
- **Geometry:** One rule line; one rhombus or square intersecting it, split-state.
- **Negative space:** The un-attested half is void; the attested half is solid.
- **Construction:** Grid-aligned; the line at a fixed proportion of total height.
- **Memorability: 3** — conceptually sharp but visually quiet; risks reading as "shape with an underline."
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Weak — the split state is lost; reads as a dot on a line.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral.
- **International:** Universal metaphor (crossing a line = commitment) — but near-universal in contract-signing UI clichés too.
- **Copyability:** HIGH — trivially approximated.
- **Risks:** (a) Reads as UI element, not brand mark; (b) "signature line" adjacency — the exact dyadic-workflow cliché we reject; (c) weak standalone presence.

### F-07 — The Registration Notch
- **Concept:** A single bold vertical bar with one precise triangular notch cut from its edge. The notch is the record of contact — proof the die passed here. Tamper-evidence as a single gesture.
- **Mechanism:** *Calibrated interruption.* A perfect form bearing exactly one deliberate, measured flaw — the flaw is the evidence (T-06's registration logic elevated to brand level).
- **Geometry:** Monolinear bar; one 45°-derived triangular cut at a fixed grid position.
- **Negative space:** The notch is a void bitten from solid — miniature deboss.
- **Construction:** Bar proportions and notch position both locked to nuqta grid units.
- **Memorability: 4** — extremely reducible ("the bar with the bite"); the deliberate imperfection is a strong mnemonic hook.
- **Scalability:** Superb — two gestures total.
- **16px:** Excellent — bar + notch survives at favicon size.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral; echoes scribal tally/register marks without depicting them.
- **International:** Universal; calibration/registration language is global in print, surveying, and engineering.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM-LOW — a single notch is simple, but *owning* "the precisely flawed bar" as a system (notch position as data, notch angle at 45°) is defensible.
- **Risks:** (a) May read as incomplete, broken, or a loading state; (b) so minimal it risks invisibility next to louder competitor marks; (c) bar-with-cut adjacency to existing minimalist marks requires trademark search discipline.

### F-08 — The Locked Ledger
- **Concept:** A stack of horizontal register lines in which one entry is offset and locked — the closed, attested record among open lines.
- **Mechanism:** *The closed entry.* Archival register (T-02) where state change is shown as one line that differs from the rest.
- **Geometry:** 3–5 horizontal bars; one indented/solid/terminated differently.
- **Negative space:** Inter-line rhythm; the locked entry interrupts the rhythm.
- **Construction:** Bars on grid; offset = one nuqta unit.
- **Memorability: 3** — the idea is good but stacked bars are the most generic geometry in UI (hamburger menu adjacency).
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Fair — reads as lines with a dot.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Ledger/register resonates with *sijill* tradition structurally.
- **International:** Universal ledger reading.
- **Copyability:** HIGH — bar stacks are unownable.
- **Risks:** (a) Hamburger-icon collision is fatal at app-icon size; (b) reads as data/table, i.e., T-06 UI layer, not primary brand; (c) bureaucratic coldness.

### F-09 — The Aperture (Closure Ring)
- **Concept:** A ring of faceted blades rotated into closure around a central point — completion as the act of closing.
- **Mechanism:** *Mechanical closure.* Finality expressed as a ring that has rotated shut; the sealed state is the closed state.
- **Geometry:** 6–8 trapezoidal facets in rotational symmetry; small central polygonal void.
- **Negative space:** Central void + inter-blade gaps.
- **Construction:** Radial division of a circle; facets on grid angles.
- **Memorability: 3** — striking, but aperture/iris marks are heavily occupied (camera, Chrome, Cisco adjacency).
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Fair — blades merge into a ring with a hole.
- **Monochrome:** Good.
- **Cultural:** Rotational geometry risks reading as decorative Islamic rosette — the exact cliché we must avoid.
- **International:** Universal, but skews "camera/optics."
- **Copyability:** HIGH.
- **Risks:** (a) Camera-aperture reading dominates; (b) rosette adjacency triggers the decorative-ornament exclusion; (c) motion (closing) is the only thing that carries the "finality" story — static form under-delivers.

### F-10 — The Faceted Die
- **Concept:** A single faceted polygonal die — the matrix itself, shown as a cut, light-catching solid.
- **Mechanism:** *The instrument as icon.* Brand the private key, not the proof: the hard object that creates truth.
- **Geometry:** Octagonal/emerald-cut silhouette with 2–3 internal facet lines.
- **Negative space:** Minimal; facet lines divide the solid.
- **Construction:** Bezel contour on grid; facets at 45°.
- **Memorability: 3** — handsome but reads as the *object*, and the object is adjacent to the excluded category.
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Fair — facet lines vanish; becomes a plain octagon.
- **Monochrome:** Fair — loses the light-catching logic without tonal modeling.
- **Cultural:** Signet-stone adjacency is intended heritage — but facet lines push toward *gemstone*, and jewelry references are explicitly excluded.
- **International:** Gem/crystal reading in the West; prestige-stone reading in MENA.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM.
- **Risks:** (a) **Jewelry/gemstone reading — brushes the strict exclusion**; (b) brands the private instrument rather than the public proof, strategically backwards (the deboss, not the die, is the ownable object per `09-SEMIOTIC-CENTER.md`); (c) needs tonal rendering, violating flat-first discipline.

### F-11 — The Interruption
- **Concept:** A uniform field of fine vertical lines with exactly one gap — and the gap itself is shaped (a diamond). The attested event as a shaped silence in the record.
- **Mechanism:** *Shaped absence in a continuum.* The witness event is what the continuous record had to stop for.
- **Geometry:** Parallel hairlines; one diamond-shaped void.
- **Negative space:** The entire concept is a void.
- **Construction:** Line pitch and void both on grid.
- **Memorability: 3** — beautiful and quiet; quiet is the problem at brand scale.
- **Scalability:** Poor downward — hairlines vanish.
- **16px:** Poor — field collapses to grey.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral; faint guilloché/security-print adjacency (acceptable, forensic-adjacent).
- **International:** Universal.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM.
- **Risks:** (a) Fails the favicon test outright; (b) works better as a *pattern/texture* in the system than as the primary mark; (c) too subtle for institutional authority at small sizes.

### F-12 — The Crease
- **Concept:** A plane bearing a single permanent fold — irreversible deformation as state change.
- **Mechanism:** *Irreversible crease.* A fold cannot be un-made; permanence through material memory.
- **Geometry:** Square plane, one diagonal crease, half-planes distinguished.
- **Negative space:** None inherent — relies on tonal distinction.
- **Construction:** Diagonal at 45° on grid.
- **Memorability: 2** — the idea is strong verbally but nearly invisible as flat vector.
- **Scalability:** Poor.
- **16px:** Poor — reads as a square with a diagonal line.
- **Monochrome:** Weak — needs tone to distinguish the folded half, drifting toward skeuomorphism.
- **Cultural:** Neutral.
- **International:** Universal (folded paper = handled document).
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM.
- **Risks:** (a) Forces tonal/skeuomorphic rendering — the exact trap `13-DESIGN-PREPARATION.md` §2 warns about; (b) "folded document" is a desktop-icon cliché; (c) weak flat behavior disqualifies it as primary.

### F-13 — The Misregistration Pair
- **Concept:** Two identical squares, offset by exactly one grid unit. Alignment is the valid state; the visible offset is the proof that registration was checked.
- **Mechanism:** *Registration as verification.* Print-trade logic: the proof of precision is the measurable offset.
- **Geometry:** Two squares, one translated diagonally by one nuqta unit.
- **Negative space:** The overlap zone.
- **Construction:** Pure grid translation.
- **Memorability: 2** — at a glance it reads as a printing error, not a mark.
- **Scalability:** Fair.
- **16px:** Poor — reads as a smudge/bug.
- **Monochrome:** Good.
- **Cultural:** Neutral.
- **International:** Print-trade audiences get it; general buyers see a mistake.
- **Copyability:** HIGH.
- **Risks:** (a) **Reads as a defect** — catastrophic for a trust brand at first glance; (b) requires the *story* to rescue the *form*, which is backwards; (c) overlap marks are common in fintech.

### F-14 — The Split Monolith
- **Concept:** A solid rectangular monolith with one hairline horizontal split, the upper half shifted by a single unit. The structure holds — but the split is registered, measured, permanent.
- **Mechanism:** *Registered fracture.* Tamper-evidence shown as a controlled, measured displacement within an otherwise unbroken mass (T-06's Indelible Monolith).
- **Geometry:** Solid rectangle; one horizontal shear with one-unit offset.
- **Negative space:** The hairline split.
- **Construction:** Split position and offset on grid.
- **Memorability: 3** — strong, monumental presence; the shear is a real gesture.
- **Scalability:** Good.
- **16px:** Fair — reads as a split block; the offset is lost.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral; monumental/institutional.
- **International:** Universal.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM.
- **Risks:** (a) Fracture reads as *broken*, not *verified* — the wrong side of the tamper-evidence story for a top-level brand; (b) aggression/litigious tone risk (excluded posture); (c) better suited to the forensic UI layer than the master brand.

### F-15 — The Axis Witness
- **Concept:** Two mirrored chevrons — Party A and Party B — face each other across a strong, impartial central vertical axis. The axis is the third party: it does not move, it does not take sides, it makes the symmetry — and therefore the agreement — possible.
- **Mechanism:** *The impartial axis of symmetry.* The witness is depicted as the structural condition that lets two parties mirror each other: neutrality rendered as geometry, with no eye, figure, or scale.
- **Geometry:** Central vertical bar; two identical mirrored chevrons pointing inward toward it.
- **Negative space:** The channels between chevrons and axis — the space the witness holds open.
- **Construction:** All elements on grid; bilateral symmetry enforced by construction, not by eye.
- **Memorability: 4** — "two parties and the line between them" narrates itself; the tripartite structure is legible without explanation.
- **Scalability:** Excellent — three strokes and a bar.
- **16px:** Good — bar with two ticks remains legible.
- **Monochrome:** Native.
- **Cultural:** Neutral-positive; arbitration/balance semantics without scales-of-justice cliché.
- **International:** Universal — every legal culture recognizes the neutral third position.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM — mirrored-chevrons-around-a-bar is approximable, but the specific proportions + the "axis as witness" system story give defensible ground.
- **Risks:** (a) Bracket/code adjacency (`> | <`) — developer-tool reading; (b) symmetry can feel static/heraldic; (c) chevrons risk "arrow/UI chrome" reading if weighted wrong.

### F-16 — The Indent (Pressure Dimple)
- **Concept:** A circle pressing into a straight line, deforming it downward into a dimple. Contact leaves a permanent, measurable deformation.
- **Mechanism:** *Pressure deformation.* The most physics-honest rendering of the deboss: force applied, substrate permanently changed.
- **Geometry:** Circle + deformed line (one smooth displacement).
- **Negative space:** The dimple void.
- **Construction:** Displacement depth on grid.
- **Memorability: 3** — tactile and true, but reads as a diagram of an event rather than a mark.
- **Scalability:** Fair.
- **16px:** Poor — dimple vanishes; circle-on-line remains.
- **Monochrome:** Fair — deformation needs either tone or contour nuance.
- **Cultural:** Neutral.
- **International:** Universal physics.
- **Copyability:** MEDIUM.
- **Risks:** (a) Diagrammatic, not iconic; (b) curve-vs-line rendering flirts with skeuomorphic shading; (c) weak small-size behavior.

## 4. Independent Ranking — Top 5

### 1. F-01 — The Pressed Nuqta (Deboss Field)
The only family that unifies all three strategic tiers in one gesture: the deboss (PRIMARY) is the pressed cell, the nuqta grid (SECONDARY) is the substrate itself, and the single-state-change logic prefigures the forensic UI (PRODUCT) without borrowing its aesthetics. Name-independent, monochrome-native, lowest copyability, infinite upward scalability. **This is the strongest hypothesis on the board.**

### 2. F-05 — The Void Core
The most radical and most durable reduction of the semiotic center: the deboss as pure negative space. Best-in-class 16px and monochrome behavior, zero cultural risk, survives any name. Weaker than F-01 only because the mark alone is mute — it needs system behavior (motion, applications) to narrate "witness."

### 3. F-15 — The Axis Witness
The clearest visualization of the actual strategy — impartial third party — with no forbidden imagery. The tripartite story is legible in the form itself. Ranked below the two deboss families because it illustrates the *role* rather than the *ownable object* (the deboss), and bracket/code adjacency needs management.

### 4. F-02 — Matrix & Impression (Dual-State Pair)
The most faithful translation of the semiotic center's private/public key physics. Held back from the top three by two-part-mark recall cost, small-size faceting loss, and stamp-skeuomorph adjacency — but as a *system* gesture (die/impression pairs across applications) it may be the richest of all.

### 5. F-07 — The Registration Notch
The reductive outlier: one bar, one deliberate flaw, flawless favicon behavior. The "calibrated imperfection as evidence" idea is genuinely fresh in the category. Ranked fifth because its minimalism risks both invisibility and an "incomplete/broken" misreading — it is the highest-variance hypothesis and worth testing precisely because of that.

**Held in reserve:** F-03 (strong but most copyable), F-14 (right idea, wrong tier — belongs to forensic UI), F-06 (threshold logic may survive as a *layout* behavior rather than a mark).

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (10 lines)

1. Sixteen genuinely distinct symbol families were generated, each defined by a unique semiotic mechanism — not cosmetic variation.
2. Thirteen directions (ring, wax, eye, scales, brackets-as-brand, monograms, calligraphy, fingerprint, hex-grid, globe, chain, hourglass) were eliminated early against the strict exclusions and hierarchy lock.
3. Every surviving family is name-independent, monochrome-native, and free of shields, locks, keys, checkmarks, and blue-gradient security clichés.
4. Rank 1: **F-01 Pressed Nuqta** — the only family unifying deboss, nuqta grid, and state-change logic in one ownable gesture; lowest copyability.
5. Rank 2: **F-05 Void Core** — the deboss as pure negative space; the best 16px and monochrome behavior of all families.
6. Rank 3: **F-15 Axis Witness** — the clearest non-literal rendering of the impartial third party; the tripartite strategy made geometric.
7. Rank 4: **F-02 Matrix & Impression** — the most faithful private/public key translation; richest as a system gesture, costlier as a standalone mark.
8. Rank 5: **F-07 Registration Notch** — highest-variance hypothesis; radical minimalism with flawless favicon behavior and a fresh "calibrated flaw as evidence" idea.
9. Six rough monochrome SVG studies (families 01, 02, 03, 05, 07, 15) were delivered to `design/phase-3/svg/`, all labeled EXPLORATION STUDY — NOT FINAL and validated as clean editable vectors.
10. Recommendation to the studio: stress-test F-01 and F-05 at 16px and in motion first; run F-15 and F-02 through the Brand Critic for bracket/skeuomorph adjacency before any convergence decision.
