# 06 Validation Plan: Buyer Personas

## Objective
Address the risk of "Strategy by Assumption" by identifying the core strategic hypotheses that require empirical validation from actual enterprise buyers before committing to the brand positioning.

## 1. Enterprise Buyers (COO / LOB Leaders)
- **Assumption to Validate:** Enterprise buyers will value and pay a premium for "officiality," "defensible proof," and "quiet authority" over cheap, fast, frictionless workflow tools.
- **Interview Focus:** 
  - How do they currently handle high-risk digital agreements?
  - Is dispute resolution/evidence a primary pain point, or is speed the only metric they track?
  - Does the concept of a "Digital Seal" resonate as a premium upgrade to an "e-signature"?

## 2. Legal / Compliance Stakeholders (GC / DPO)
- **Assumption to Validate:** The legal buyer will perceive Khatim's evidentiary weight and compliance posture as a primary differentiator against legacy players.
- **Interview Focus:**
  - What makes a digital signature "trustworthy" in their eyes?
  - Does the brand's proposed tone ("exact, evidence-led, composed") build institutional confidence, or does it feel like unnecessary friction?
  - How do they evaluate the risk of current lightweight SaaS signers?

## 3. IT / Security Stakeholders (CISO / CTO)
- **Assumption to Validate:** IT/Security leaders will view Khatim as true infrastructure rather than just another SaaS application, and will value its architectural rigor.
- **Interview Focus:**
  - What specific technical certifications or architectural patterns signal "infrastructure-grade" trust to them?
  - How do they perceive the gap between their current PKI infrastructure and end-user signing tools?
  - Would they integrate a third-party "trust API" into their internal applications?

## 4. Government / Institutional Buyers (MENA & Western)
- **Assumption to Validate:** The Arabic cultural heritage of the "Khatam" (seal) will be perceived as a sign of deep structural trust and authenticity, rather than a localized ethnic niche or tokenism.
- **Interview Focus:**
  - (MENA): Does the branding feel authentic and institutional, or does it conflict with colloquial meanings (e.g., jewelry)?
  - (Western): Does the cultural origin story add premium value (like a Swiss watch), or does it create confusion/friction?
  - Can Western buyers pronounce the name without significant hesitation?
