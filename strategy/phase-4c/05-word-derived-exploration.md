## 1. The word's three loaded structural facts (ختم / كَاتِم head sense)

1. **The kha crown-dot (I'jam).** خ (kha) is ح (ha) plus ONE dot ABOVE. The dot is the *information bit*: its presence-and-position changes the letter (and thus the word). خاتم **starts** with this dot — the brand name is literally built on "the point that decides identity." It is the word's attestation operand.
2. **The alif pillar + tā' twin teeth.** The word's only three vertical escapes from the horizontal Arabic stream are the alif pillar, the tā' twin teeth, and the crown of dots (1 over خ, 2 over ت, 0 over م). A horizontal, dot-textured rhythm with sparse vertical accents.
3. **The falling seal-tail (م).** Only the final م descends below the baseline; its closed bowl + descender "falls and seals" the word — root ختم = *seal + complete + reach the end*. The word's END is a descent/completion.

**Begin = a witnessing dot. End = a sealing fall.** The word encases its own story.

## 2. Can the word generate the mark? Yes — and it already did (F1)

The cross-glyph **F1 "Witnessed Letter"** is the word-derived outcome:
- From خ: the **one crown-dot** (the I'jam) is the mark's load-bearing dot.
- From the Latin **K**: the shared stem + two diagonals give the bilingual skeleton (phase 4B handoff priority #1).
- The **sealing-tail** is retained as a *system* behavior (motion: dot seats = seal; and a descending counter-stroke available in the wordmark), not forced into the 16px mark.

F1 therefore is not a "logo inspired by Arabic" — it is a cross-glyph built on the *actual first-letter operand* of the word. This satisfies the **cultural test** (a deep Arabic-typography observer recognizes the I'jam logic structurally) and the **international test** (reads as bold abstract lettering unknowing the name). It is name-agonistic (survives a Codegic fallback name) while remaining structurally Arabic.

## 3. What was rejected (avoid tokenism)

- ❌ Calligraphic flourish of خاتم — decorative, culturally tokenizing, and the most saturated e-sign register.
- ❌ Arabic placed beside Latin (pairing is fine for the wordmark; not a *symbol* generator).
- ❌ Latin decorated with Arabic ornaments (D-003 reject).
- ❌ The whole word as the mark — too detailed for 16px; a word is for the lockup, not the favicon.
- ❌ The dot-field / crown-of-dots as the mark — the commodity dot/grid register.
