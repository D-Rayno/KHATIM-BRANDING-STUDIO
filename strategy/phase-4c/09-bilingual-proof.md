# PHASE 4C — 09 Bilingual Proof

> **Date:** 2026-08-27
> **Scope:** Evaluate خاتم / KHATIM as one brand system across all lockup configurations.

---

## Principle
The Arabic version is not a decorative translation. It is a **structural co-equal**. The mark (F1) is literally the first letter of the Arabic word, constructed as a cross-glyph. The Latin wordmark extends the same stem geometry. Both scripts share one visual DNA.

---

## Optical Weight & Rhythm

| Configuration | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Arabic-only** | خاتم reads right-to-left with a horizontal baseline rhythm. The word's natural density is higher than KHATIM (4 letters vs 6, but Arabic letters are wider when connected). | Balanced |
| **Latin-only** | KHATIM is all-vertical strokes + one diagonal (K). The stem of F1 extends naturally from the K. | Balanced |
| **Bilingual LTR** | KHATIM left, symbol center, خاتم right. The symbol acts as a pivot. Stem weight of F1 (32px at 256) matches medium-weight wordmark stems. | Balanced |
| **Bilingual RTL** | خاتم right, symbol center, KHATIM left. The symbol remains the pivot. RTL reading is natural for Arabic-first contexts. | Balanced |

---

## Alignment & Scale

- **Symbol-to-wordmark ratio:** Symbol cap-height = wordmark cap-height. The dot of F1 sits at the ascender line, creating vertical extension that prevents the lockup from feeling squat.
- **Baseline:** Latin baseline and Arabic baseline are optically aligned (Arabic baseline sits slightly lower; compensated by the symbol's vertical center).
- **Spacing:** The symbol's stem creates a natural "wall" that separates and joins the two scripts. Minimum clearspace = 1× stem width on all sides.

---

## Cultural Authenticity

- The Arabic wordmark uses a **geometric sans skeleton** (not calligraphic flourish) to match the F1 construction. The خ in the wordmark echoes the symbol's خ-reading.
- The Latin wordmark uses a **geometric sans with sharp terminals** to match the 45° arm angles of F1.
- No ornaments. No "Arabic-style" Latin. No "Latin-style" Arabic. Both are modern geometric constructions that share the symbol's DNA.

---

## International Usability

- Non-Arabic viewers see a bold monogram + Latin wordmark. The Arabic is present but not dominant.
- Arabic-first viewers see their script given equal weight and structural integration.
- The symbol bridges both audiences without explaining itself.

---
