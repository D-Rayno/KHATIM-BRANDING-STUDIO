## Why F1 Wins

### 1. The protecting idea is visible at rest, uncaptioned
The gate reviewer from Phase 4B demanded: "the protecting idea must be present *in the resting silhouette itself* — visible, uncaptioned, not reducible to 'cut corner + dot.'"

F1 satisfies this:
- The silhouette is a single bold glyph that reads as **both K and خ**.
- The dot sits **above the stem at the I'jam register** — a position that is structurally specific to Arabic typography.
- A viewer who knows Arabic sees the dot that turns ح into خ. A viewer who does not sees a distinctive crown dot on a bold letterform.
- The meaning is **positional**, not decorative. Remove the dot → different letter (unwitnessed). Add the dot → witnessed. This is visible in the form.

### 2. It departs the saturated container register
F1 is **not** a square, octagon, diamond, grid, or seal-container. It is a letterform-composite — a category that the competitive red team identified as under-claimed in trust infrastructure (vs. saturated geometric containers).

### 3. It passes all four critical tests

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **2-Second Test** | "Angular K with a crown dot" — memorable and distinguishable from cybersecurity/fintech/AI marks. |
| **16px Test** | Stem (4px) + arms (diagonal mass) + dot (~2px) all survive. The dot may become 1px at favicon size, but the K-silhouette remains distinctive. |
| **Wordless Test** | The mark has character without text — bold, asymmetric, crowned. |
| **Cultural Test** | A deep Arabic-typography observer recognizes the I'jam logic structurally. The dot is not explained afterward; it IS the graphemic operation. |
| **International Test** | Reads as a bold abstract monogram to non-Arabic viewers. No Arabic knowledge required. |
| **Competitor Test** | DocuSign cannot launch this without looking like a copy of Khatim. The specific K∩خ union + I'jam dot position is delineable and protectable. |

### 4. It is name-agnostic
If legal clearance forces a name change, the cross-glyph K∩خ survives as a structurally Arabic mark (the first letter of any name derived from the ختم root). The mark is tied to the **root**, not the specific word "Khatim."

### 5. It is the word-derived outcome
F1 is not "inspired by Arabic" — it IS the first letter of خاتم, constructed as a cross-glyph. The word-derived exploration confirms this is the inevitable form.

## Why F3 is Rejected

- "Triangle + dot" is saturated (mountain, location pin, upward arrow).
- The witness-triad concept is invisible without caption.
- No Arabic structural connection.
- Generic at silhouette level.

## Why F7 is Reserve (Component Only)

- Convergence knot is philosophically strong but visually generic (network node / radiation).
- Better deployed as a **pattern element** or **document texture** than as the resting mark.
- The asymmetric convergence logic can seed a secondary pattern family.

---

## Why F9 is Reserve (Behavior Only)

- Best expression of the verification-state toggle (dot present/absent).
- Wrong format for a single mark (diptych).
- Deploy as the **product UI behavior** (before/after attestation animation), not the brand symbol.

---

## Why No Secondary Mark is Selected

The brief demands "no hybrid of everything." F1 is a coherent, complete idea. Adding a secondary mark would dilute the system and create decision fatigue. The reserve candidates (F7, F9) are not marks — they are **system behaviors/patterns** that F1 generates.

---

## The Honest Risk

F1's genus (letter + verification signifier) has incumbents: DigiCert (D+check), DocuSign (D+agreement). The defense is not the premise but the **execution**: the dot is graphemic (I'jam crown), never a check/tick. This positional specificity is the moat. If the dot ever drifts to a check position, the mark collapses into generic e-sign territory. This risk is managed by **canonical geometric lock** in the master SVG.

*End of selection rationale.*
