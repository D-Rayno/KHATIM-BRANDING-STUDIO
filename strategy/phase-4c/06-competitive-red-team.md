## 2. The cliché register checklist (attack-on-sight)

- **Generic geometry:** polygon containers (hex/shield/diamond/star), boolean-cut primitives, "made in Figma" negative-space cuts in a box.
- **Tech clichés:** diagonal offset panes, stacked/layered squares, chevrons, angle brackets, code glyphs.
- **Seal clichés:** stamp face, badge, crest, wax seal, circular seal, rosette.
- **Cyber clichés:** shield, padlock, key, check-in-shield, hex, chain.
- **Fintech clichés:** interlocking blades, kinetic swash, gradient globe, "transaction node" dots.
- **AI-logo clichés:** asymmetric geometric abstraction, polyformed blobs, gradient iridescence, pure symmetric rotated-shape marks.
- **Arabic-design clichés:** calligraphic swoosh, arabesque corners, ornament flourishes, orientalist crescent/lamp/kufic-box. Anything decorative rather than typographic.
- **Startup-template:** generic mark + wordmark from a generator.

## 3. Risk of the K∩خ cross-glyph letterform direction

### Premise is published (not proprietary)
- **Alkhafrah** (The Generator, UAE investments) is explicitly "an integration of Arabic (خ) and Latin (K) letterforms." Not in trust/e-sign category → not a blocking trademark for us, but the *premise* is not novel.
- **Jhapa Khabar** (Nepal) fuses Latin K + Devanagari ख — same family of move. "Latin consonant + sibling-script letter fusion" is a known designer move.
- **So: differentiate on EXECUTION and semantic, never claim the premise.**

### Genus collision in-category
- **DigiCert** (D+check single stroke) and **DocuSign** (D+agreement overlap) both live in "Latin letter fused with a trust/verification signifier." A K + dot can be filed under the same genus.
- **The dot is the entire exposure.** If the nuqta reads as a checkbrush/swoosh or sits where a tick goes, viewers file us under "letter + verification mark" next to DigiCert, in the most crowded e-sign cliché.

### AI-reproducibility
- A generator prompted "bilingual Arabic/Latin K monogram" produces a triangular K + خ-bowl + dot as a **top-10 output** — the "Latin-meets-Arabic monogram" is overtrained in AI datasets (Looka/Brandmark/etc.), and neogrotesque bilingual fonts (Tafkir, Zetafonts) already match K/خ construction.
- **Do not assume "too clever to copy."** Protection comes from a canonical, exacting geometric construction + the semantic dot argument — not from picking an underexplored idea.

### Copyable as concept, strong as identity
- Geometric containers: most saturated, least defensible.
- Letterform fusion: the *specific union* into one silhouette + a load-bearing dot is concrete and delineable (examiner-protectable) — DigiCert/DocuSign/OneSpan all prove letter-fusion holds registerable equity.
- **But the concept is AI-reproducible** → the moat is the exacting geometry + semantic, not the idea being rare.

### Verdict
Strong and on-brand ("trust decided by a single point" — perfect for attestation). Not collision-free. **The single lever that decides the fate is the nuqta: keep it graphemic (the نقطة that MAKES خ), load-bearing, unmistakably a dot of the letter — NEVER a verification tick.** The moment it reads as a check, we become category-generic.

## 4. The 5 most dangerous registers to avoid
1. **The verification-tick dot** (SignNow, DigiCert, GlobalSign, Yousign) — our nuqta is structurally adjacent to a check; #1 existential risk; one bad placement = collapse into e-sign trust cliché.
2. **Signature flourish / pen-nib swash** (Skribble, SignNow, Yousign, OneSpan) — most saturated e-sign register.
3. **Letter + trust-signifier overlap** (DigiCert, DocuSign) — we are already in this genus; don't push into exact-category overlap.
4. **Geometric container + gradient** (Entrust hex, Docaposte pellets, AI iridescence) — template/cyber tell; strips typographic dignity.
5. **Calligraphic/orientalist decoration** — cheap Western-Arabic tell; buries letterform under ornament. Keep it typographic; the dot is the only non-letter element.

---

**Consequence for Phase 4C design:** the cross-glyph (K∩خ) direction is the right one, but it must be executed so the fused silhouette reads as **one piece of lettering solving into a single point** — and the point must be unmistakably the graphemic نقطة, never an affirmation mark. This is now a hard design constraint.
